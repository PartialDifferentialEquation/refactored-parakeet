01 Roles

Five jobs, ten seats

Every seat is named and rostered. If a responsibility below has nobody’s name against it on the day, that is a hole in the event, not a detail to sort out later.

Range 3

Range Engineering

Owns the fifteen target services end to end: build, deploy, flag rotation, the synthetic probe fleet that measures the Service Floor, and the flow-record pipeline competitors read on delay.

  • Range build and freeze, T−2 weeks
  • Flag placement and rotation schedule
  • Service Floor probes and their calibration
  • Sudden Death rotation on Day 7
  • On call for Range faults, always

Infra 2

Gateway & Infrastructure

The three VLANs, the switch, the OPNsense baseline Blue inherits, the capture chain, and the competitor image. Holds switch management credentials, which the Blue Faction never does.

  • VLAN 10 / 20 / 30 and their ACLs
  • Golden image build and per-station verification
  • Capture path monitoring — alerts on non-NDI traffic
  • VPS pool allocation to Red teams
  • Spare switch, spare laptops, spare everything

Rules 2

Arbitration & Scoring

The referees. Score reconciliation, Disclosure Order adjudication, scope rulings, and disqualification calls. At least one arbiter is on duty at every moment of the event.

  • Rulings within 15 minutes of a call
  • Daily score publication and dispute window
  • Disclosure Order issue and verification
  • DQ calls — requires two arbiters to concur
  • Rulings log, published daily

Broadcast 2

Production

The desk. Production switching, commentary, overlays, the watch handover segments, and the VOD archive. The producer on duty holds the kill switch and needs nobody’s approval to use it.

  • 60-second delay, kill switch on the desk
  • Three handover segments daily
  • Scoreboard and gateway overlays
  • Credential and personal-data spotting
  • VOD cut and publication after the event

Welfare 1

Welfare & Floor

Rest enforcement, first-aid liaison, the quiet room, catering timing, and one of the two named code-of-conduct contacts. Has the authority to pull anybody off the floor, including crew.

  • Badge-enforced 8-in-24 rest compliance
  • First-aid responder liaison
  • Code of conduct contact
  • May stand anybody down, without appeal
  • On floor 08:00–24:00, on call otherwise

Note

Ten is the floor, not the target

Ten seats is the minimum that covers 168 hours without anybody working unsafe hours. If the event grows past thirty competitors, Range Engineering and Arbitration are the first two to add to.

Crew roles are compensated — travel plus an honorarium. This is not a volunteer event pretending otherwise.

03 Runbook

The shape of a day

Days 2 through 6 run identically. Day 1 adds arrival and handoff, Day 7 replaces the noon escalation with hourly rotation and closes at 13:00.

00:00

Dawn watch handover — producer cuts to it live. Arbiter observes and logs anything contested.

02:00

Range snapshot and scoring database backup. Verify the flow-record pipeline is still emitting on the one-hour delay.

08:00

Day watch handover, on air. Welfare lead comes onto the floor.

11:00

Range health check across all fifteen services. Stage tomorrow’s flag rotation. Confirm probe fleet calibration.

11:45

Escalation brief to the Blue Faction — what unlocks at noon, and what it does not include.

12:00

Capability layer goes live. Infra confirms the new rules are actually loaded before anyone announces it.

12:15

Verify Service Floor probes still read true against the new layer. A misreading probe costs Blue 500 points unfairly.

16:00

Night watch handover, on air.

Rolling

Adjudicate Disclosure Orders as issued. Reconcile scores continuously. Answer scope questions on demand.

23:00

Publish the day’s scores and the rulings log. Opens the 24-hour dispute window.

Range fault versus Blue action

The distinction that generates the most disputes. If a target becomes unreachable because the Range broke rather than because Blue blocked something, the affected Red teams have their bridge-hours credited back for the outage window and Blue’s Service Floor is not penalised.

Range Engineering declares faults; Arbitration applies the credit. Both entries go in the rulings log the same day. Never resolve one of these quietly — the appearance of a silent correction is worse than the fault.

04 Arbitration

How calls get made

Competitors are building things nobody has seen before, at speed, for a week. They will find edges in the rules. The job is not to have anticipated every edge — it is to rule quickly, consistently, and in public.

Standing procedure

  • Any competitor may call for a ruling at any hour
  • Arbiter on duty responds within 15 minutes
  • Scope questions asked in advance are always free — never penalise someone for checking first
  • Disqualification requires two arbiters to concur
  • Every ruling is logged and published the same day
  • Scoring disputes: 24 hours from publication

Precedent

  • A ruling binds the rest of the event, for every team
  • If a ruling turns out wrong, correct it publicly and credit affected teams
  • Never make a novel ruling that changes standings without both arbiters
  • Where the rules are silent, rule for the more interesting game
  • Where safety is involved, rule for safety and explain afterwards

Disclosure Orders

Blue issues up to three a day. Arbitration serves the order, starts the ten-minute clock, and verifies the disclosure against what the gateway and the VPS pool actually show. A team may refuse once per event at a cost of 300 points, and the refusal is announced on the broadcast.

False disclosure is disqualification — and it is the one DQ that will be contested, so verify against two independent sources before either arbiter says the word out loud.

05 Incidents

When something goes wrong

Four categories, four different first calls. Everyone on crew should be able to recite these without looking them up, because the ones that matter happen at 03:00.

Medical

Named first-aid responder first, always. Welfare lead informed immediately. Emergency services without hesitation if there is any doubt — the broadcast is not a consideration.

  • Producer cuts the stream, no approval needed
  • Game clock does not stop; scoring is adjusted after

Code of conduct

Two named contacts, published with applications. One is the welfare lead; one sits outside the White Team entirely, so no report has to run through the people running the event.

  • Reports never go through the reporter’s own faction
  • Welfare lead may stand anybody down immediately

Technical

Range Engineering declares whether it is a fault or a legitimate block. Infra owns anything below the gateway. Credit affected teams and log it the same day.

  • Spare switch, spare gateway, spare laptops on site
  • Scoring database snapshotted every two hours

Broadcast

Credentials, personal accounts, faces that should not be on camera. The 60-second delay exists exactly for this and the producer uses it without asking anyone.

  • Cut first, discuss after — never the other way round
  • Log every cut so the VOD edit knows where to look

Scope violations are a fifth case and they are handled quietly and fast: Infra confirms from the gateway logs, both arbiters review, and the team is told before anything is said on air. A competitor accused of pointing tooling outside the Range in front of a live audience — wrongly — is a serious harm we do not get to take back.

Four open decisions

City and dates — every cost is a placeholder until fixed. Venue — 24-hour access is the hard requirement and it eliminates most rooms. Pilot or flagship first — the highest-leverage call available, and the recommendation is the pilot. The name — cheap to change now, expensive later.

07 Join

Crew applications

What we need

People who have run something operational at three in the morning and can say clearly what happened afterwards. Range and Infra seats want hands-on build experience; Arbitration wants judgement and the temperament to be unpopular for good reasons.

Apply to crew

What it involves

Part-time commitment through the build, full-time for the event week plus setup and teardown. Compensated — travel, accommodation and an honorarium. You do not compete, and you do not get to place bets on the outcome either.

  • Build phase from TBC
  • On site 9 days including build and teardown
  • Same 8-in-24 rest rule as competitors
