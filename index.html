01 The work

Two jobs, seven days

You apply to one side. Red Factions build proxy bridges into the Range and keep them alive; the Blue Faction holds the gateway everything crosses. Both are real jobs with real shifts, and neither of them requires you to be awake at four in the morning.

The infrastructure runs for 168 hours. You don’t. Bridges score at 1.5× while no member of your team is on the floor. The single most profitable thing you can do all week is build something that holds without you — and then leave it alone.

Offense 18 places

Red Faction

Six independent teams of three. You get a laptop, a slice of the VPS pool, and a gateway that hates you more every day. Build transports, capture flags, and keep endpoints alive through six escalations.

  • Build and operate proxy bridges into the Range
  • Capture flags across five difficulty classes
  • Keep bridges live and undetected — that is the score
  • Compartmentalise as slots open, D2 through D6
  • Answer Disclosure Orders truthfully, or pay to refuse
  • Work whatever hours you like

Defense 12 places

Blue Faction

One team on the OPNsense gateway, in three watches. You are not trying to switch the network off — you are trying to find six teams inside it while keeping fifteen services usable.

  • Hold an 8-hour console watch, same slot all week
  • Hunt anomalies, fingerprint TLS, read flow records
  • Block, then file the IOC inside 15 minutes
  • Hold the Service Floor at 95% availability
  • Issue up to three Disclosure Orders a day
  • Brief the incoming watch, live on air

02 The Range

Fifteen services, all ours

The Range is the entire target space and the entire permitted space. It is built and hosted by the White Team, it is the only thing the gateway will route to, and it is designed so that every technique in the event has something honest to be used against.

Content & apps

  • 4× static content hosts
  • 3× authenticated web apps — session handling under inspection
  • 3× rate-limited JSON APIs

Pressure targets

  • 1× bulk file service — exists so throttling matters
  • 2× fronting targets, organiser-hosted
  • 2× rotating hosts — live for Sudden Death

How it behaves

  • Flags rotate on a published schedule
  • Flow records released on a 1-hour delay
  • Synthetic probes run against all fifteen, continuously
  • Frozen two weeks before the event

Flag classes

Nothing outside the Range is a target — not the public internet, not any third-party service, and not any real censorship system. The gateway enforces a destination allowlist, so out-of-scope traffic cannot leave even if you try: scope is a property of the network, not of your good behaviour. Techniques that would otherwise require abusing somebody else’s infrastructure — domain fronting among them — are played against targets we host for the purpose.

How Sudden Death works

At the top of every hour the Range publishes its next target set to the scoreboard. The catch: a rotated target is reachable only through a bridge that was already live before the rotation, and new bridges take twenty minutes to begin scoring.

You cannot rebuild your way through Day 7. The teams still standing at 13:00 are the ones that spent six days engineering for persistence instead of chasing captures.

The Service Floor

Blue has to keep the Range usable while defending it. Synthetic probes run continuously against all fifteen services from a reference client; if availability drops below 95% in any rolling hour, Blue forfeits that day’s Service Floor award.

Switching everything off is not a win, it is a forfeit — exactly as it would be for any defender who is not permitted to take the business offline to stop an intruder.

Disclosure Orders

Three times a day, Blue may compel one Red team to disclose the full configuration of one named bridge endpoint, truthfully, within ten minutes. You may refuse once per event at a cost of 300 points, and the refusal is announced on the broadcast. False disclosure is disqualification.

05 Your kit

What we hand you

Everything is standardised, so nobody wins on equipment and nobody has to spend Day 1 installing things. Bring yourself and a change of clothes.

The machine

A common event laptop from a single golden image, yours for the week and wiped after.

  • Linux — every tool here is native to it
  • Wireshark, tcpdump, Zeek, Suricata
  • Docker, Python, Go, build chain
  • Transport suite and WireGuard
  • Editor of your choice, pre-installed

The desk

A fixed station on the floor with your faction, powered and cabled, available all week.

  • External monitor, keyboard, mouse
  • Wired to VLAN 10 only
  • Physical privacy cut for capture
  • Locker for personal devices

Off the floor

A room ten minutes away and food that isn’t only caffeine.

  • Single occupancy by default
  • Sharing opt-in, with a named partner
  • Three hot meals a day
  • 24-hour coffee, snacks, actual water
  • Quiet room, off camera

Infrastructure

Red teams get their own slice of the event’s hosting to build endpoints on.

  • Allocation from the event VPS pool
  • Subdomains under the event domain
  • Scoreboard and flow-record API access
  • Bridge slots unlock D2–D6

06 Rules of engagement

The short version

The full handbook ships with your acceptance. These are the parts that end someone’s week, so they are here in plain sight before you apply.

Disqualifying

Don’t

  • Aim any traffic outside the Range
  • Use the capture network to move game traffic — it is watched
  • Attack another competitor’s machine or their bridges
  • Answer a Disclosure Order falsely
  • Attack event scoring, the broadcast chain, or the White Team
  • Put personal accounts on event hardware

Expected

Do

  • Take your 8 hours off-floor in every 24 — it is required
  • Ask an arbiter if you are unsure whether something is in scope
  • Report anything that looks like a Range fault, not exploit it silently
  • Publish your write-up after the event, if you want to
  • Use the reporting path in the code of conduct if you need it

On audits: inspections cover event-issued hardware and competitor desks only. They are announced, conducted by named White Team staff with you present, and never extend to persons or personal belongings. In-game infrastructure disclosure is handled entirely by Disclosure Orders, which is a scoring mechanic you can refuse.

On camera: any competitor screen can be cut to live. Capture is opt-in at registration, indicated on screen whenever it is running, cut by you at the station whenever you want, and delayed 60 seconds with a producer kill switch. Event accounts only.

Do I have to stay awake for 168 hours?

No, and you are not allowed to. Every competitor is off-floor for eight hours in every twenty-four. The format is built so this costs you nothing: Red bridges score 1.5× while their team is away, and Blue watches hand over to a rested crew.

What does “bridge-hours” actually mean?

Ten points for every hour a bridge is live, carrying real traffic, and undetected by Blue — multiplied by 1.5 for any hour your whole team is off the floor. A bridge that survives one night unattended is worth more than several captures.

Can I apply alone for Red?

Yes. We place solo applicants into teams during selection, and we ask about working style so the pairings are not random. Applying as a complete team of three is simpler for everyone, though.

What if I have never done defensive work?

Blue is the harder application, not the easier one, but the skill we look for is judgement under noise rather than tool familiarity. The gateway stack is documented in advance and there is a briefing day before the game opens.

Can Blue just block everything?

It would lose them the week. The Service Floor requires the Range to stay above 95% availability in any rolling hour; below that, Blue forfeits the day’s award. They have to defend the network without taking it away.

Will anyone search me or my belongings?

No. Inspections cover event-issued hardware and competitor desks only, are announced in advance, and happen with you present. In-game disclosure is handled by Disclosure Orders, which you can refuse.

Can I write about it afterwards?

Yes, and we would like you to. Everything you build is yours. The White Team publishes a post-mortem and the flow records after the event so write-ups can be specific.
