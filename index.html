01 The format

Not a weekend CTF

A normal capture-the-flag ends before anyone has to live with their architecture. Censorfest runs for a full week, in one venue, with standardised hardware and 24-hour access — long enough that a clever hack written on Tuesday has to survive until Sunday against a defender who gets stronger every single day.

Red Factions build resilient proxy bridges, defeat deep packet inspection, and extract flags from blocked targets. The Blue Faction runs the gateway everything routes through. The White Team writes the rules, arbitrates, and puts the whole thing on air.

All flag targets are infrastructure operated by the event. Competitors never point tooling at third-party services, and no real-world censorship system is a target. Full rules of engagement ship with the competitor handbook.

02 Factions

Three roles, two directions

Scoring is asymmetric by design. The factions are not playing the same game — they are playing against each other’s definition of winning.

Offense

Red Faction

Small independent teams building proxy bridges, tunnelling through active DPI, and pulling flags off blocked targets. Getting through once is easy. Staying through is the game.

Team size  2–4 players Flag captures  50–200 pts Daily uptime bonus  for bridges that survive undetected

Defense

Blue Faction

One unified team holding the event’s OPNsense gateway. They read traffic anomalies, fingerprint TLS, and write drop rules — but they score for the evidence, not the block.

Team size  3–5 players Points for  IOC submissions filed before blocking Also scores  audit-round findings

Control

White Team

Organisers and game masters. They own the infrastructure, enforce the rules of engagement, arbitrate disputes, and run the broadcast desk around the clock.

Role  Non-competing Owns  targets, scoring, arbitration Runs  the live production

04 Architecture

Two networks, one venue

The Blue Faction is expected to break things. That is the point — which is why the thing they can break is kept strictly away from the thing that keeps the event running.

VLAN 10

The Game Network

Every competitor packet routes through the Blue Faction’s OPNsense gateway. This is the battleground: inspected, throttled, sinkholed and blocked in real time.

  • OPNsense gateway on dedicated appliance
  • Suricata and Zeek from Day 3
  • Full Blue Faction visibility and control
  • Carries all flag traffic

VLAN 20

The Production Network

Strictly whitelisted and out of play. Staff telemetry, internal chat and the video feeds live here. The Blue Faction has no visibility and no control over it.

  • Broadcast video and staff telemetry only
  • Egress locked to the production hosts
  • No competitor-routable path to the internet
  • Switch management held by White Team only

Competitor machines contribute a video feed to VLAN 20 through a one-way capture path. There is no general-purpose route from a competitor workstation onto the production network — using the broadcast path to move flag traffic is disqualifying, and it is instrumented.

05 Broadcast

Every screen is on air

Censorfest is produced like a match, not a meetup. A commentary desk runs off an OBS production rig that can cut to any competitor’s screen live, with shoutcasters calling the network as it happens.

What goes out

Competitor screens, gateway dashboards, the scoreboard, and desk commentary — mixed live and streamed for the full seven days.

  • Multi-source production switcher

Competitor protections

Being on camera for a week is a real commitment, so the controls around it are explicit and they belong to the competitor.

Do I need to stay awake for 168 hours?

No. The event runs continuously; competitors do not. Scheduled rest windows are part of the format and no scoring mechanic rewards skipping sleep. Beds are ten minutes away and yours for the week.

What hardware do I bring?

None. Every competitor gets a standardised event laptop from a common image with the toolchain pre-installed, so nobody wins on equipment. Personal devices stay off the game network.

What exactly are the targets?

Web services built and hosted by the White Team for the event. Nothing on the internet at large is in scope, and directing tooling at anything outside the target range is an immediate disqualification.

Is my screen really on the stream?

Any competitor screen can be cut to live, which is why capture is opt-in at registration, indicated on-screen whenever it is running, and cuttable by you at the station. Log into event accounts only.

Is accommodation included?

Yes — lodging for the full week, three meals a day, and a 24-hour supply of coffee and snacks. Room-sharing arrangements are confirmed with you before the event, never assigned blind.

What are the rules on physical audits?

Audit rounds cover event-issued equipment and competitor desks only. They are announced, conducted by named White Team staff, and never extend to persons or personal belongings.
